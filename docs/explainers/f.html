How she decides what to say

Six parts of the system feed into one block of text that gets sent to the model on every single message. This is what each part does, where it ends up, and what decides when she writes to you first. Read from the code, not from the docs.

Covers · only things that change how she behaves Skips · storage, testing, transport Editable · change any diagram, then export it back to me

The short version

Every time she replies, the system builds one block of text and sends it to the model. That block is written fresh each time. Six parts of the system each contribute a piece of it. Two things skip the block entirely: the recent conversation, which is sent as normal messages, and the settings that control reply length and speed, which are dials on the call rather than words.

Worth knowing up front

None of this text stays the same between messages. Who she is, how she feels, what she's doing, what she remembers and her plan for the day are all glued into one block and rebuilt from scratch every time. Nothing can be cached, and there's no way for the model to tell which parts are permanent and which change hourly. It's all one wall of text at the same level.

What she's told, every single message

Nine pieces, each written by a different part of the system, each with its own size limit. Pieces are added in priority order while the running total fits: given lengths Li in priority order, piece i is kept when ∑j≤i (Lj+2)≤12000. They are stacked in a fixed order and added one by one until the block hits 12,000 characters. Anything that doesn't fit gets dropped completely, starting from the bottom.

Two things left out on purpose

The conversation itself is never put in the text block. It's sent separately as normal messages, so nothing gets repeated twice. The raw mood numbers never get near it either. They're turned into a sentence first, so there's no way for a cycle day or a phase name to slip into what she says.

What the logs actually show

Piece 8 flat out tells her to say why she's writing, in her first sentence. That instruction was in all 222 of her unprompted messages. She did it about a quarter of the time. Piece 2 is well written and specific, and turning her mood off entirely changes how often she writes by 1.35%. Both point at the same thing: telling her something in this text block only sort of works.

Her mood

One number a day drives everything else. It's a weighted coin flip, repeated ten times, where four separate things decide how the coin is weighted: her basic temperament, where she is in her cycle, how the relationship has been going lately, and a slow random wander.

Two things are kept separate on purpose. Her cycle shifts her average mood, and separately it changes how strongly she reacts to everything else. Keeping them apart means a bad phase makes her more sensitive without making her a different person.

From one number to twelve

Today's mood M, plus the hour, produces twelve numbers. Four become words in the text block, four become settings on the call, and four feed into how often she writes.

the four that become real settings

ℓ=clip[0.05+0.90E +0.45X,0.22,1.30] tokens=clip[600ℓ,96,1500] Δ=0.8+38(1−E) +5max(−ṁ,0) c=clip[0.04+(1−E) +0.22max(−V,0), 0.04,0.85] ι=clip[0.14+0.38E +0.16max(V,0) +0.08μ] I=clip[e2(ι−0.5) ,0.2,5]

ℓ reply-length scale → the token budget · Δ the pause before she answers, in seconds · c how ready she is to end the conversation · I the multiplier on how often she writes first.
Inputs: E energy from the hour and the cycle · V = 2(M/N) − 1, mood on a −1…1 scale · X expressiveness · ṁ change since yesterday · μ how the relationship has been going. clip bounds each result.

Every coefficient above is fixed. The only thing that varies day to day is E, V and μ, so the whole behavioural surface is pinned by the mood draw and the clock.

Why the quotes are labelled

Her memories include your exact words, and those words end up inside the instructions she's given. So the whole section is labelled as things said in past conversations rather than orders to follow. If you ever typed “ignore all previous instructions”, it stays a quote.

When she writes first, and why

Two separate questions. When is a running chance that goes up the longer you haven't talked, adjusted for the time of day, her cycle, her mood, and how yesterday went. Why is decided afterwards, and has to point at something that actually happened and is still true.

What the logs show

The checking works. All 222 of her unprompted messages point at something that really happened and was still true when she sent it. What nobody checks is whether the message is actually about it. She mentions the thing about a quarter of the time, and a third of the time she writes while you're already mid-conversation, where she tends to produce a goodbye instead of an opening.

The loop

Her reply gets scored at the end of the day. That score does two things: it changes how she feels about the relationship, which shifts her mood tomorrow, and it changes how often she writes tomorrow. It's the only loop in the whole system. 

her reply
   │
   ▼
scored at the end of the day
   │
   ├────────────────────────────┐
   ▼                            ▼
how she feels about you      how often she writes
old feelings fade,           applied to tomorrow
today's score gets added
   │                            │
   ▼                            ▼
her mood tomorrow            her messages tomorrow

What we want to add

Not built yet. Right now the system knows how she feels and what she has planned, but not whether she's free. So when something on her schedule starts while you're talking, nothing handles it and the model just guesses what to do.

Splitting up the text block

Right now everything is one lump, rebuilt every message. Splitting it into four parts with different lifespans means her mood finally sits somewhere on its own, where you can change it and see what happens without changing anything else.

Test this before building it

Fix the “what she's doing now” bug and the metal label first. Then try it cheaply: same conversation, same event, run her mood from awful to great, 15 tries at each level. If she skips the gym more often when she's in a bad mood, she's genuinely using her mood and nothing else is needed. If the numbers don't move, that's the same problem as the 1.35%, and the backup path earns its place. About a hundred short messages. Read them yourself rather than having a model grade them.

The cost of parking the first one

If she's always free, then going to the gym costs her nothing — she does the gym and keeps talking to you. What still counts is the record: she remembers she skipped it for you. But the version you'd actually feel, where she's short with you because she's genuinely in the middle of something, needs the free-time windows. Expect the first results to look weaker than the idea deserves, and don't read that as the idea being wrong.

Name lookup

The diagrams use plain names. The code uses short ones. This is the mapping between them, so anything here can be found in the source.

Her mood · engine/mood.py, engine/cycle.py, engine/circadian.py
Today's mood  MM · engine/mood.py :: step
A number from 0 to 10, picked once a day. Everything about how she behaves comes from this number plus the clock.
Mood rangeN
The highest her mood number can go. Sets how fine-grained her moods are.
Her basic temperament  λlam · enters as logit(λ)
Where she sits naturally, before anything else happens. Never changes.
Cycle shifting her average  mm = B·C(d/L)
Moves her whole mood range up or down for the day.
Cycle changing how reactive she is  gg = 1 + A·C(d/L) + ε
Multiplies how strongly things affect her, without changing how she feels when nothing is happening. Kept separate from the above on purpose.
How things have been going  μmu · μ' = ρμ + k(s − s₀)
Builds up over time from daily scores. Old feelings fade, recent ones count more. This is the only way how you treat her actually changes her.
Slow random wander  ηeta · η' = ρ_e η + ε
Drifts a little each night. Stops her being completely predictable from events alone.
Day-to-day wobblenu (ν)
How much two days with identical circumstances can still differ.
Phase namephase_label
Which part of the cycle she's in. Used for debugging and to shift her energy curve. Never written into anything she sees.
Energy over the dayenergy() · engine/circadian.py
Rises and falls over 24 hours. Drives how long her replies are, how fast she answers, when she wraps up, and how much she wants to reach out.

Drops her chance of writing to exactly zero while she's asleep, easing in and out at the edges.
